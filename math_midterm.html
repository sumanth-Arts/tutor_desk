Number Systems, Surds & Values

Understanding the foundations of numerical reasoning, including rational/irrational numbers, surds, and estimation.

Key Definitions

  • Rational Numbers: Can be written as a fraction \(\frac{p}{q}\) where \(p, q\) are integers and \(q \neq 0\). Includes integers, terminating decimals, and recurring decimals.
  • Irrational Numbers: Cannot be written as a simple fraction (e.g., \(\pi\), \(\sqrt{2}\)). Their decimal expansions are non-terminating and non-recurring.
  • Absolute Values: The distance of a number from zero on the number line. \(|x| = x\) if \(x \geq 0\), and \(|x| = -x\) if \(x < 0\).

Surds & Recurring Decimals

To convert a recurring decimal (like \(0.777...\)) to a rational fraction, set \(x = 0.777...\), multiply by a power of 10 to shift the decimal (\(10x = 7.777...\)), and subtract to solve for \(x = \frac{7}{9}\). Surds are irrational roots (e.g., \(\sqrt{3}\)) that must often be rationalized in denominators.

Estimation, Approximation & Scientific Notation

Scientific notation writes numbers as \(A \times 10^n\) where \(1 \leq A < 10\). Estimation requires rounding values to an appropriate number of Significant Figures before calculating.

03

Exponents & Logarithms

Laws of Exponents

\[ a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n} \quad | \quad \frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n} \quad | \quad (a^m)^n = a^{mn} \quad | \quad a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n} \]

These laws are used to solve exponential equations, often by finding a common base (e.g., \(2^x = 8 \Rightarrow 2^x = 2^3 \Rightarrow x = 3\)).

Logarithms

Logarithms are the inverse of exponents. If \(a^b = c\), then \(\log_a(c) = b\).

05

Polynomials & Quadratics

Factor & Remainder Theorems

  • Remainder Theorem: If a polynomial \(P(x)\) is divided by \((x - a)\), the remainder is \(P(a)\).
  • Factor Theorem: If \(P(a) = 0\), then \((x - a)\) is a factor of the polynomial. This establishes the idea of zeroes and its role in factorisation.
  • Rational Root Theorem & Fundamental Thm of Algebra: Helps identify potential rational roots and confirms an \(n\)-degree polynomial has exactly \(n\) roots (complex or real).

Nature of Roots in Quadratic Equations

For \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\), the discriminant \(\Delta = b^2 - 4ac\) determines root nature:

  • \(\Delta > 0\): Two distinct real roots
  • \(\Delta = 0\): One repeated real root
  • \(\Delta < 0\): No real roots (complex roots)

Vieta's Theorem

Defines the relationship between roots (\(\alpha, \beta\)) and coefficients of \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\):

\[ \text{Sum of roots: } \alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a} \quad | \quad \text{Product of roots: } \alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a} \]

Applying these concepts helps in forming and solving complex equations in word problems of Polynomials degree two.

06

Graphical Representation & Simultaneous Equations

Understanding the graphical representation of Linear, Quadratic and Simultaneous Equations.

Conditions of Solvability of Simultaneous Equations

For a system of linear equations \(a_1x + b_1y = c_1\) and \(a_2x + b_2y = c_2\):

  • Unique Solution (Intersecting lines): \(\frac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \frac{b_1}{b_2}\)
  • No Solution (Parallel lines): \(\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \frac{c_1}{c_2}\)
  • Infinite Solutions (Coincident lines): \(\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2}\)

Midterm Practice Examples

1. A polynomial \(P(x) = 2x^3 - kx^2 + 5x - 6\) leaves a remainder of 4 when divided by \((x - 2)\). Find the value of \(k\).
Cr: A
Model answer Using the Remainder Theorem, \(P(2) = 4\).
\(P(2) = 2(2)^3 - k(2)^2 + 5(2) - 6 = 4\)
\(16 - 4k + 10 - 6 = 4\)
\(20 - 4k = 4 \Rightarrow 4k = 16 \Rightarrow k = 4\).
2. The roots of the quadratic equation \(3x^2 - 7x + 2 = 0\) are \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\). Calculate the value of \(\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta}\).
Cr: A
Model answer Using Vieta's Theorem, Sum of roots \(\alpha + \beta = -\frac{-7}{3} = \frac{7}{3}\).
Product of roots \(\alpha\beta = \frac{2}{3}\).
We need \(\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta} = \frac{\alpha + \beta}{\alpha\beta}\).
Substitute the values: \(\frac{7/3}{2/3} = \frac{7}{2}\).
3. Investigate the pattern for the sequence 3, 8, 15, 24, 35... and determine its \(n\)th term formula.
Cr: B
Model answer Find the first differences: \(8-3 = 5\); \(15-8 = 7\); \(24-15 = 9\); \(35-24 = 11\).
Find the second differences: \(7-5 = 2\); \(9-7 = 2\); \(11-9 = 2\).
Since the second difference is constant, it is a quadratic sequence of the form \(an^2 + bn + c\).
\(2a = 2 \Rightarrow a = 1\).
\(3a + b = 5 \Rightarrow 3(1) + b = 5 \Rightarrow b = 2\).
\(a + b + c = 3 \Rightarrow 1 + 2 + c = 3 \Rightarrow c = 0\).
The formula is \(U_n = n^2 + 2n\).
